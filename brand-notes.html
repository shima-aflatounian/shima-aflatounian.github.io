How brands decide what they're worth.

I grew up in a market where jewellery is bought by weight — the jeweller weighs it, applies the day's gold price, and that is the value. Then I spent years selling fine jewellery in a market where most of the price is the name on the box.

That gap is what I write about here: what makes someone believe a thing is worth what it costs. Each entry starts with a question I can't already answer, separates evidence from interpretation, and ends with a decision someone could act on.

Shima Aflatounian · Brand & consumer strategy · Los Angeles

How each entry is built

The same chain every time. The value isn't the conclusion — it's whether each link actually holds.

01

Question

One sentence, genuinely open. If I already know the answer it isn't a question.

02

Claim

What the brand says about itself, in its own words.

03

Evidence

Verifiable facts, each attached to a source. Marked in rust throughout.

04

Tension

The conflict inside one customer's head, not between two companies.

05

My read

A position, stated plainly, with what would prove it wrong. Marked in green.

06

So what

One recommendation, and what it would cost or earn.

Case 01 — Jewellery · Premiumisation

Pandora and the price of meaning

Pandora is moving upmarket while its like-for-like growth falls to 2%. Is the premium push a brand strategy, or a margin rescue?

What the company says

The stated strategy

Pandora's 2025 report sets out four growth pillars: Brand, Design, Markets and Personalisation. The company states it wants to stimulate demand rather than simply expand distribution, with particular emphasis on North America, where it reports growth below expectation.

Lab-grown diamonds sit inside the Design pillar, alongside a stated shift away from many incremental product extensions toward fewer, higher-impact launches.

Five-year summary, p.14 · like-for-like growth, %

Pandora is guiding to a year of decline. 2026 guidance is organic growth of −1% to +2%, with like-for-like of −3% to 0%. EBIT margin is guided down to 21–22% from 23.9%.

Almost all of the guided revenue growth comes from opening stores, not from selling more per store.

Outlook, p.15

The premium segment is not yet the growth story. The Core segment grew 1% like-for-like in 2025. Fuel with more — which contains Lab-Grown Diamonds and ESSENCE — grew 3%.

The company targets mid-to-high single digit LFL CAGR for that segment. It delivered 3%.

Growth Pillar 2: Design, p.20–21

Silver is the pressure behind the strategy. The price of silver more than doubled in 2025, reaching USD 72/oz by year-end against USD 29 at the start of the year and USD 24 when long-term targets were set in 2023. Commodities, FX and US tariffs cost 220bps of gross margin and 300bps of EBIT margin.

Letter to Shareholders, p.6 · Phoenix, p.20

The platinum shift is explicitly about reducing silver exposure. From 2026 Pandora introduces platinum-plated jewellery on its EVERSHINE alloy, stating improved everyday-wear performance compared to silver and high consumer acceptance in testing. Parts of the silver assortment will gradually shift across, with the stated aim of "reducing silver exposure and commodity sensitivity over time."

Growth Pillar 2, p.21–22

The loyalty base is unusually large for jewellery. My Pandora launched 2022, now live in seven countries with 16.2 million members, covering more than half of revenue in participating channels.

Engraving capacity grew by 855 machines during 2025 to a total of 2,290, with 34 additional engravable pieces added.

Growth Pillar 4: Personalisation, p.22

Scale, for context. 915 million visitors to stores and online in 2025. 112 million pieces crafted. More than three pieces of jewellery sold every second. Presence in over 100 countries.

The conflict

Tension

Draft — to be confirmed or replaced by the reviews. I bought into Pandora because it was affordable and it was mine. But the last piece I owned lost its colour within a year. So when they show me a diamond, I don't think "finally, one I can afford" — I think "will this one last either?"

Test this

This came from my own experience, which makes it a hypothesis rather than a finding. If thirty reviews don't surface something like it, rewrite it to match what people actually say. Do not keep a tension because it sounds good.
