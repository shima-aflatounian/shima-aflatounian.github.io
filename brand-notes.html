All eight are built on one brand, Pandora, using only its published 2025 Annual Report and publicly observable market information. Where a claim rests on an assumption rather than evidence, it is marked as one.

01 Core PMM artifact

Positioning statement

What it is: five lines that fix who a product is for, what category it competes in, the single benefit it owns, and the proof. Everything downstream — messaging, campaigns, sales scripts, packaging — inherits from this. If two people on a team write different versions, the company has a positioning problem, not a marketing problem.

Pandora · Lab-grown diamonds

For women who buy jewellery for themselves and already own several Pandora pieces,
Pandora Lab-Grown Diamonds is the first diamond that fits inside a collection rather than replacing it,
that lets a milestone be marked without a proposal, a partner, or a five-figure decision,
because it is sold by the brand she has already trusted with the pieces she wears daily, at prices that start where other diamond retailers end,
unlike traditional jewellers, who sell diamonds as an event, and online lab-grown specialists, who sell them as a spec sheet.

What this rests on Pandora's report states the brand targets consumers seeking precious-metal jewellery "that carries personal meaning for the wearer, offered at accessible price points," and reports 16.2m loyalty members. The self-purchase emphasis is the company's own framing. The claim that existing owners are the highest-intent diamond segment is my hypothesis and would be validated with purchase-sequence data from the loyalty base.

What it is: the category on one page, compared on the dimensions customers actually choose between — not on feature lists. A good matrix makes the white space visible without arguing for it.

The read. Every competitor above Pandora on price acquires a customer at the moment of purchase. Pandora is the only one that already has a multi-year relationship before the diamond conversation starts. That is the asset the positioning should be built on — and it is not a price advantage, which is where the category usually competes.

The exposure. Mejuri occupies the same self-purchase occasion with a more contemporary design language and no charm-bracelet association to overcome.

03 Customer research

ICP & persona

What it is: the ICP is the firmographic or behavioural filter used for targeting — who to spend money reaching. The persona is the human underneath it, used for writing. Teams confuse them constantly. You target an ICP; you write to a persona.

Ideal customer profile — targetable

  • Behaviour: 3+ Pandora purchases in 24 months
  • Basket trend: average order value rising year over year
  • Engagement: loyalty member, opens email, has used engraving
  • Channel: shops both store and online
  • Trigger: purchased within 60 days of a personal date

Every line above is a filter that can be applied to the existing loyalty database. That is what makes it an ICP rather than a description.

Persona — writable

She buys her own jewellery and has stopped apologising for it. Early thirties. Bought her first Pandora piece as a gift from someone else, then kept buying. The bracelet marks things — a job, a trip, a year she got through.

A diamond has always meant someone else's decision and someone else's money. She is not waiting for that any more, but a jeweller's counter still feels like a room she has to be prepared for.

What she wants: to mark something significant, in a place she already feels competent.

What stops her: whether a Pandora diamond counts as a real one — to her, and to anyone who notices it.

Validation required The persona's central objection — whether the piece "counts" — is hypothesis, drawn from category behaviour rather than from Pandora customer data. Validating it requires review analysis and, ideally, ten customer interviews. It is stated here as a bet, not a finding.
04 Message architecture

Messaging hierarchy

What it is: one primary message, three supporting pillars, and a proof point under each. It exists so that a social post, a sales script and a product page all say the same thing in different lengths. Without it, every channel invents its own story.

Primary message

Some things you don't wait to be given.

Everything below supports that sentence. Nothing that contradicts it ships.

It belongs to what you already own

Designed to sit alongside pieces she has collected for years, not to replace them. The diamond is an addition to a story already in progress.

PROOF · Lab-grown diamonds sit inside the existing design language and retail experience, alongside collections she already buys.

It's a real diamond, on her terms

Chemically and optically identical to mined. The difference is origin and price, not substance.

PROOF · Pandora moved to lab-grown exclusively in 2021, positioning the material as a deliberate choice rather than a substitute.

It's made to be worn, not stored

Built for daily wear rather than for occasions. The everyday-wear claim is the durability answer and the usage answer at once.

PROOF · From 2026, platinum-plated jewellery on the EVERSHINE alloy, stated as improved everyday-wear performance versus silver, with high acceptance in consumer testing.

What we deliberately do not say

  • "Affordable diamonds." Price-led framing invites comparison against specialists who will win it, and it undercuts the meaning claim.
  • "Just as good as mined." Defensive. Positions the product as a substitute.
  • "Treat yourself." Trivialises a purchase the persona experiences as significant.

A messaging hierarchy that only lists what to say is half-finished. The exclusions are what keeps thirty people on-message.

05 Sales enablement

Competitive battlecard

What it is: a one-page reference for whoever faces the customer. In software it goes to sales; in retail it goes to the store colleague. It must be honest about where you lose, or nobody trusts the part about where you win.

Where we win

  • She's already a customer. No trust to build, no education on the brand. The conversation starts at "what for" rather than "who are you."
  • The price removes the ceremony. She can decide alone, today, without it being an event.
  • Personalisation. Engraving is available in-store and the capability is now at scale — 2,290 machines and 34 engravable pieces added in 2025.
  • She can hold it in an hour. Online specialists cannot.

Where we lose

  • Engagement. We are not in that consideration set and should not fight for it.
  • Carat-for-dollar. A specialist will beat us on stone size at equivalent spend. Do not compete here.
  • "Is it a real diamond?" The brand association works against us on first exposure.
  • Gifting by a partner. He goes to a jeweller. We are not on his list.

Objection handling

She saysShe meansResponse
"Is it a real diamond?" Will anyone be able to tell? Same material, same optical properties, grown rather than mined. Show the certification, then move to the piece.
"I could get bigger elsewhere." Am I overpaying for the name? Don't defend the carat. Move to what it's being bought for — this is a piece that joins what she already wears.
"My last piece tarnished." Will this last? The strongest objection and the honest answer is the new material. Platinum-plated everyday-wear performance is the response — but only once it ships.
"I'll think about it." This feels like a big decision. Reduce the ceremony rather than pushing. Offer engraving as the next step — it converts a purchase into a decision about meaning.
06 GTM planning

Launch one-pager

What it is: the document that gets a launch out of the building. One page, because anything longer will not be read by the people whose agreement is required.

Platinum-plated collection · 2026

WhatPlatinum-plated jewellery on the EVERSHINE alloy, replacing parts of the silver assortment. In-market pilot first, broader launch later in the year.
Why nowSilver more than doubled during 2025 to USD 72/oz; 2026 planning assumes USD 82/oz. Commodity, FX and tariff pressure cost 220bps of gross margin and 300bps of EBIT margin.
Business goalReduce silver exposure while improving the durability perception that limits how far the brand can move upmarket.
AudienceExisting owners first — they are the ones who have experienced silver ageing and will notice the difference.
Core messageMade to be worn, not stored.
ChannelsStore colleagues first (they handle the objection daily), then loyalty email, then product pages, then paid. Deliberately not a brand campaign.
Success metricsAttach rate to existing customers · repeat purchase within 90 days · durability mentions in reviews · share of new pieces sold in the new material
Biggest riskFraming it as a cost measure. If the story becomes "cheaper for us," the durability benefit is lost and the trade press writes the margin story instead.
Decision neededWhether to name the material change publicly or ship it silently inside existing lines.
Source: Pandora Annual Report 2025 — Letter to Shareholders p.6, Phoenix p.20, Growth Pillar 2 p.21–22, Outlook p.15.
07 Commercial strategy

Pricing & packaging read

What it is: where the product ladder helps or blocks the customer moving up it. Pricing is usually treated as a finance question; the part product marketing owns is whether the steps make sense to a human.

The gap in the ladder

A charm costs tens of dollars. A lab-grown diamond piece starts in the low hundreds. That is roughly a tenfold step, and there is very little between the two that teaches a customer to spend more.

Why it matters commercially. Core grew 1% like-for-like in 2025. Fuel with more, which contains the diamond line, grew 3% against a mid-to-high single digit target. A customer who has never spent above $80 at a brand is being asked to make a tenfold jump in a single decision.

The structural question. Packaging can build the step the ladder is missing — a mid-tier entry that makes the diamond the second upward move rather than the first. That is a packaging decision, not a discount.

What I would not do. Discount the diamond line to close the gap. A brand whose growth already depends on store openings rather than like-for-like cannot afford to teach its customers to wait for markdowns.

08 Stakeholder communication

Executive update memo

What it is: 300 words that reach a decision. The skill is subtraction — an executive memo that explains the analysis has failed. It should open with the recommendation and let the reasoning follow.

To: VP Marketing
From: Product Marketing
Re: Sequencing the premium push
Decision needed: Whether to lead 2026 with diamonds or with material

Recommendation: lead with the material change, not the diamond line.

Three facts sit together uncomfortably. Like-for-like growth fell to 2% in 2025 and is guided to −3% to 0% for 2026. Almost all remaining growth comes from opening stores rather than selling more within them. And Fuel with more, the segment carrying our premium ambition, grew 3% against a mid-to-high single digit target.

The diamond line is not failing for lack of awareness. It is being asked to carry a tenfold price step from a customer base whose most common experience of our product is a piece that ages visibly. We are marketing permanence to people we have taught to expect impermanence.

The platinum-plated launch addresses that directly, and consumer testing already shows high acceptance. Sequenced first, it earns the durability claim that the diamond line needs in order to justify its price. Sequenced second, we spend 2026 defending a premium product with a material story we have not yet told.

Ask: approval to reallocate Q1 launch support from the diamond line to the material rollout, targeting existing customers first. Diamond support resumes in Q2 with the durability proof in hand.

What would change this: if loyalty data shows diamond buyers are predominantly new customers rather than existing ones, the durability objection is not the binding constraint and the sequencing should reverse.
