Keysight Technologies · Demand generation

GTM funnel & demand analytics

Keysight's marketing organisation had a target: grow sales-qualified leads 10% year over year. Through FY22 the pipeline was clearing that target comfortably. At the same time, sales kept rejecting marketing-qualified leads and marketing kept generating more of them.

Both things were true, which meant the headline number was hiding the actual constraint. I worked the funnel backwards to find where qualified demand was being created, where it was being lost, and whether the regions producing it matched the regions the business wanted to grow.

01 · The GTM challenge

Hitting the target and missing the problem

Measured against the year-to-month goal, qualified pipeline value ran between 121% and 126% every single month from March through July 2022. That consistency is itself the finding: a funnel that beats its target by roughly the same margin for five straight months is not being driven by anything marketing changed month to month.

Qualified pipeline value against year-to-month goal Measured

FY22 year-to-date value as a percentage of the year-to-month goal, where the goal was set at a 10% increase over FY21. Indexed to goal; absolute values are withheld.

A flat 20-point cushion across five months points to a goal calibrated against the wrong baseline, not to marketing performance improving. It also meant nobody upstream was being asked why so much marketing-qualified volume never became pipeline at all.

02 · The funnel leak

The leak was in qualification, not in selling

I pulled every rejected marketing-qualified lead with its stated rejection reason and regrouped the reasons by who could act on them. That regrouping is the whole insight: the losses were not spread evenly across the organisation, and almost none of them were a sales execution failure.

Lead readiness

Owned by nurture strategy
  • Not sales ready
  • Timing
  • Need not established

The lead is real, but it was handed over too early to be worked.

Data & targeting

Owned by campaign design
  • Incomplete details
  • Wrong persona
  • No buying authority
  • Duplicate lead

The form captured someone who was never going to be able to buy.

Process & timing

Owned by marketing operations
  • Unreachable, no response
  • Routed incorrectly

The lead was valid and was simply never properly worked.

Read this way, the funnel's problem becomes legible. Marketing was measured on volume of qualified leads, so it optimised for what produced volume — gated content downloads and event registrations. Those channels reliably produce contact records. They do not reliably produce people with a need, a budget and authority, which is exactly why the rejection reasons cluster on readiness and targeting rather than on selling.

The recommendation that follows is uncomfortable but simple: the qualification definition was the defect. A bar set at "downloaded something" guarantees a rejection queue that no amount of sales follow-up can clear.

03 · Regional demand

Demand sat in three markets. The plan assumed more.

If we were going to tighten qualification, we needed to know which markets could absorb it — so I looked at where web engagement was concentrated across the region, using three-month rolling averages to strip out campaign spikes and reporting noise.

Web visits by market, three-month rolling average Measured

Rolling average of monthly web visits per market as of July 2022. Rolling averages are a smoothing method here, not a finding in themselves.

France, the United Kingdom and Germany accounted for the overwhelming majority of engagement, each an order of magnitude above the next tier. India registered roughly 2,600 visits against France's 132,000 — a ratio that does not resemble the market opportunity and looks far more like a measurement or coverage gap than a demand gap.

That distinction is commercially decisive. If the number is real, India is being under-invested in. If it is a tagging or domain-attribution problem, part of the region's demand has been invisible to planning for years. Either answer changes where budget goes, and it costs almost nothing to check before spending anything.

04 · GTM recommendations

What marketing and sales should do differently

Redefine what qualifies a lead

Move the bar from content consumption to demonstrated fit and intent. Persona and authority signals belong inside the qualification rule, not in a rejection reason recorded after sales has already spent time on the record.

Stop rewarding volume alone

A qualified-lead count with no acceptance rate attached will always drive the wrong behavior. Pairing volume with a sales-acceptance rate makes the cost of loose qualification visible to the team creating it.

Route by readiness instead of rejecting

The largest recoverable group is leads that were real but early. Those should return to nurture with their timing signal attached, rather than being discarded and re-acquired later at full cost.

Fix reachability before adding volume

Unreachable and misrouted leads are an operations problem with a far cheaper fix than net-new demand generation, and they sit entirely within marketing operations' control.

Verify the regional gap before reallocating budget

Confirm whether low-engagement markets reflect genuine absence of demand or a tracking gap. Small piece of work; gates a large prioritisation decision.

Rebase the target

A goal beaten by roughly 20% every month for five months is no longer steering anything. Rebasing it restores its ability to signal when something is actually going wrong.
