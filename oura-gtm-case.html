GTM brief: membership repositioning

Moving the membership from "access to your data" to "the service that keeps working"

Owner: Shima Aflatounian| Status: Draft 2, for review| Updated: 27 Sep 2026
Decision needed by: before any pricing test is scoped| Blocked on: Q1 below

Independent exercise. I don't work at Oura and have no access to their internal data. Everything here is built from published pricing, product pages and owner discussions in smart‑ring communities. Where I'm guessing, I've said so. Written in the format I'd use internally.

1.The decision on the table

Every rival ring now ships with no ongoing fee. Samsung's Galaxy Ring is $399 once. RingConn and Ultrahuman charge nothing after purchase. We're at $349 plus $5.99 a month, and the membership gates most of what the ring collected.

The reflex is to treat this as a price problem and cut the fee. I think that's wrong, and the money isn't where the damage is. Over three years we're $160 more expensive than Samsung. That's less than one replacement ring. The problem is that nobody standing in front of both rings can tell what the $70 a year buys.

So the decision isn't whether to charge. It's what the membership is understood to be.

2.What the evidence actually says

I went looking for people defending the fee, expecting to find nobody. There are more than I thought, and they don't defend it on features.

A long-term owner who has used three different rings says the sensors are basically the same across brands. Same person says Oura replaced a failed ring at no cost, and that a competitor told him he'd have to pay for a repair or a new one. His conclusion was that the subscription is cheaper than it sounds if the replacements keep coming. Another owner in the same thread prefers our interpretation because it tells her what to actually do, and describes a competitor's guidance as generic.

Two things follow. First, we are not going to win on sensors and we should stop trying. Second, the people who stay are staying for interpretation and for what happens when the hardware fails. Neither of those is "your data."

Q1 — blocking Is ring replacement actually tied to an active membership, or is it standard warranty? I can't tell from outside and the whole brief rests on it. If it's warranty, section 4 collapses and we need a different differentiator. Need Legal or CX to confirm before this goes anywhere.

What I couldn't get

No conversations with people who bought a competitor. Everything above is from owners who stayed with us, which is the sample most likely to be generous. Ten calls with Samsung and RingConn buyers would test this harder than anything else here, and I'd want them before we commit spend.

3.Recommendation

Include twelve months of membership with the ring. Keep basic tracking permanent. Renew annually after that.

This removes the objection that kills the sale at the shelf — that an expensive ring turns into jewelry when you stop paying — without conceding that the ring is the product. It also moves the renewal conversation to month twelve, when the customer has a year of their own data and has seen the thing work.

TierWhoPriceIncludes

Q2 Does including twelve months raise year-two renewal, or does it move the same churn twelve months later and cost us a year of revenue on the way? I don't know. This is the thing to test before rollout, not after.

4.Messaging

Two audiences, and they're at opposite ends of the same decision. One is comparing prices on a shelf and hasn't bought anything. The other paid a year ago and is deciding whether it was worth it. Same positioning, different message.

A — comparing at the shelf

LayerMessage
ValueEvery ring measures. Only one keeps telling you what it means.
CapabilitiesGuidance rather than readouts · works on iPhone and Android · ongoing hardware support
ProofHigher owner ratings than the subscription-free alternative at a comparable price
Objection"$70 a year on top?" → $160 more over three years, less than replacing one failed ring

B — deciding whether to renew

LayerMessage
ValueThis year, it noticed things before you did.
CapabilitiesAnomaly detection · trend interpretation · a ring that stays supported
ProofTheir own year — the alerts they got, the patterns surfaced, the support they used
Objection"I already have a year of data" → the data isn't the product, the reading of it is

Things we should stop saying

  • "More accurate." Owners say the sensors are comparable. This starts a fight we don't need and probably lose.
  • "Only $5.99 a month." Minimizing the number concedes it's a cost rather than a service.
  • "Unlock your full data." This is the paywall framing. It's the sentence that makes the fee feel like a hostage situation.

The retail line I'd actually put on the shelf card: "Ask what happens when the battery stops holding a charge." It's the one question a subscription-free competitor can't answer comfortably. Needs Legal review before it goes near a store — it's pointed.

Q3 Is the real competitor another ring, or the Apple Watch already on the wrist? One owner in the thread read every comparison and then decided to keep his watch. If that's the common path, the whole brief is aimed at the wrong fight and sleep comfort is the wedge, not interpretation. Worth a question in the next round of purchase research.
