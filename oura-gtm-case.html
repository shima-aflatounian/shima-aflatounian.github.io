GTM brief: winning back Oura owners who stopped paying

Relaunching the membership to people who already own the ring

Owner: Shima Aflatounian| Status: Draft 3, for review| Updated: 27 Sep 2026
Decision needed by: before the holdout test is scoped| Blocked on: Q1 below

Independent exercise. I don't work at Oura and have no access to their internal data. Everything here comes from Oura's published help pages and pricing, press coverage of the Ring 5 launch, and owner discussions in smart-ring communities. Where I'm guessing, I've said so. Structured on the Product School GTM template, written in the format I'd use internally.

1.Strategy: who this launch is for

Every new Oura ring comes with one free month of membership. After that it's $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year, and without it the app drops to three daily scores (Sleep, Readiness, Activity), battery and settings. The ring keeps working. It just stops explaining anything.

Oura has sold about 5.5 million rings and says it has "several million" paying members. I can't subtract those two numbers (replacements, Gen2 rings, people with two rings), but there is clearly a group of people who own a Gen3 or newer ring and aren't paying. That's the launch: bring those owners back to the membership.

The timing is what makes it a launch and not a routine email. Ring 5 shipped in June with new software (Health Radar, AI guidance built with Counsel Health, GLP-1 Insights, blood pressure signals), and Oura made those features available to members on Gen3 and newer. So someone who left a year ago now has a real reason to come back that costs them no hardware.

One more thing changed in the same window. In May, Oura launched Menopause Insights, built around its own Menopause Impact Scale, and more than 600,000 members have filled it in. In September it announced ten care partners (Alloy Health, Maven, Gennev, Tia and others) that will use the scale in their own workflows. That gives one slice of lapsed owners a much more specific reason to come back than "new features," and a channel Oura doesn't have for anyone else: their care provider.

Options considered: a Ring 5 upgrade push to Ring 4 owners (mostly a hardware launch, not a membership one) and first-time buyers comparing rings at the shelf (Draft 2 of this brief, and too close to what Oura's CEO already says publicly). I chose win-back because it's the only one where the membership itself is the thing being relaunched.

Motion

Lifecycle and product-led, not marketing-led. The hardware is already on their finger, so there's nothing to acquire. Acquisition is an in-app card and an email. Activation is wearing it five of the first seven nights back and opening one insight they couldn't see on the free tier. Monetization is the free month converting to paid, ideally annual.

One-sentence thesis

Oura will bring lapsed Gen3+ owners back to the membership through in-app and email outreach, using the new Ring 5 software and their own history as the reason, and it works if returners are still paying at day 90 at a higher rate than a holdout.

2.Competition: what a lapsed owner does instead

For a first-time buyer the fight is Oura against Samsung and Ultrahuman at the shelf. For someone who already owns the ring, it flips. The cheapest paid option is coming back to Oura, and the real competitor is paying nothing at all.

AlternativeWhat it does wellWhere it's weakHow we win

The bet

We're betting that a meaningful share of lapsed owners left because the value got fuzzy, not because they stopped caring, and that the new software plus their own history is a concrete enough reason to come back.

  • Why now: the Ring 5 software is live on Gen3+ rings, and three no-fee rivals mean a lapsed owner is one purchase away from being gone for good.
  • Not doing: discounts beyond the free month, Gen2 owners (they can't get the new software), pushing hardware upgrades, anything that sounds medical.
  • Right within 90 days if: returners are still paying at day 90 at a clearly higher rate than the holdout.

Battlecard

One-line advantage: coming back costs $70 and keeps your history; switching costs $349 or more and starts from zero.
Question to plant: "How long would a new ring take to learn what normal looks like for you?"

They sayResponse
"Three scores were enough"For a while, yes. Here's what your Readiness score was made of last week.
"I'll just use Apple Health"Don't argue. Mention what only works on Oura's side (Health Radar, Advisor) and leave it.
"I'll wait for a discount"There isn't one. The free month is the same one every new ring gets.

Evidence

  • Oura's CEO says 75% of paying members open the app at least five times a week. The people who stay use it constantly, which suggests leaving is about value, not the habit breaking.
  • In owner threads, a long-term owner who had tried three rings said the sensors are basically the same across brands; another preferred Oura's guidance because it tells her what to do. That's interpretation, not hardware.
  • Oura is building features for specific life stages, not only general scores. The menopause scale is the clearest example, and Oura is putting it in front of providers, which makes an active membership useful beyond the app.
  • If you cancel and keep your account, Oura keeps your data, and it's back as soon as you re-enroll. The history is real and it's already on their side.

What I couldn't get: cancel reasons, the size of the free-tier base, and whether Oura already makes offers in the cancel flow. I couldn't find a public win-back program, but there could be one I can't see from outside.

5.Pricing

Goal: defend the base, not grow revenue per member. Every lapsed owner who switches to a no-fee ring is gone for good; one who comes back is worth the full membership again.

Model: keep the existing subscription. The owner already paid for the hardware, so the only question is the membership, and the price isn't what I'd change.

TierWhoPriceIncludes
Free tierAnyone who owns a ring$0Three daily scores (exists today)
MonthlyReturners who want to try it$5.99/mo, first month freeFull membership
Annual ★Default for returners after the free month$69.99/yrFull membership

I'd route returners to annual after the free month. Someone who comes back monthly can leave again in 30 days; annual gives the new features a full year to prove themselves.

Research: MaxDiff with lapsed owners on which of the new features pulls them back (Health Radar, Advisor, blood pressure signals, cycle insights, Menopause Insights, GLP-1 Insights). It decides what the in-app card leads with. The price itself I'd test with a live holdout, not a survey. People who already canceled once will tell you any price is too high.

Tripwire: if returners cancel again at the same rate as they originally left, stop. We'd be paying for a month of attention and nothing else.

Sequence matters more than speed. Nothing goes out before Q1, because if most of the base is group B the whole plan becomes an email campaign to people we may not be allowed to email.

The whole thing in one paragraph

Lapsed owners are the one group for whom Oura is the cheapest paid option. The Ring 5 software gives them a reason, their history gives Oura an advantage nobody else has, and the only real competitor is doing nothing. So the launch is small, cheap and testable: one free month, two messages, one holdout, one number at day 90.

What changed in how I think about this
