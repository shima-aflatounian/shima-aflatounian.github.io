Module 1 · Growth Hypothesis & Bet

The bet evolved with evidence

Initial hunch

Collaboration could make FinWise stickier

I initially believed that prompting users to share a valuable financial insight with a bookkeeper, accountant, or teammate would improve retention by embedding FinWise in an ongoing workflow.
What changed

The constraint appears earlier

Trial-to-paid stays near 2%, one-year paid retention is 40%, and generic feature usage is not a strong enough predictor of conversion. Collaboration therefore remains a hypothesis, not a proven root cause.
Updated hypothesis

Repeat differentiated value first

If Aha-complete trial users return within seven days and review another meaningful financial insight, repeat engagement and ultimately conversion should improve because FinWise becomes an ongoing decision tool rather than a one-time dashboard.
The bet

Activation → repeat value → collaboration

Prioritize the first and second meaningful insight before buying more traffic. Test collaboration only after repeat value is established.
Deliberately not prioritizing: more paid acquisition, extra onboarding questions, pricing changes before diagnosing value vs. price churn, or treating collaboration as causal before testing it.

Module 3 · Retention & Engagement

Turn insight into a weekly financial habit

Mechanic

Cash Safety Buffer

FinWise turns financial insights into visible progress toward a meaningful business outcome, such as building a 6-week cash runway. The user sees exactly how completed actions change the buffer.
Natural cadence

Weekly review momentum

A week counts when the user reviews a fresh financial signal and takes one meaningful action. Simply opening FinWise does not count.
Habit loop

Trigger → Action → Reward → Investment

Trigger: weekly cash check-in ready.
Action: complete one recommended financial action.
Reward: see measurable days of runway gained.
Investment: saved progress becomes next week’s starting point.
Why this fits

Useful progress, not empty gamification

The mechanic does not reward logins or punish missed weeks. It makes a real business outcome visible and optionally lets users share the plan with a trusted collaborator.

Module 5 · Experimentation Plan

Validate the repeat-value hypothesis cleanly

Method · Standard user/account-level A/B test
Objective

Repeat the value moment

Increase the percentage of Aha-complete trial users who return and review a second meaningful financial insight within seven days.
Single change

Weekly Financial Check-In

Control: current experience after first insight.
Treatment: when a fresh insight is ready, surface a structured weekly check-in tied to the user’s financial progress.
Target

Aha-complete trial accounts

Users who connected data, received their first meaningful insight, and have not yet converted to paid.
Decision fieldPlan
Primary metric7-Day Repeat Financial Review Rate
Success threshold≥15% relative lift vs. control; this is a design threshold, not an observed result.
GuardrailWeekly check-in opt-out / dismissal rate must not increase by more than 5% vs. control.
Read ruleMinimum 14 days plus required sample size; balanced exposure; no colliding test; no early peeking.
If successfulRoll out incrementally, then use a longer-term holdout to test trial-to-paid conversion and 30/90-day retention.
If unsuccessfulInvestigate insight quality, cadence, visibility, fatigue, and whether the constraint still occurs before the first Aha.
Decision discipline: ship only if the primary metric clears the threshold and guardrails remain healthy; iterate for a modest win; investigate a mixed signal; stop if there is no meaningful lift after the planned read.
Module 6 · Pricing & Monetization

Diagnose before repricing

Stage 1 · Value Creation · Subscription / reverse trial
Recommendation

Do not optimize the price point yet

There is not enough evidence to recommend raising or lowering price. First prove repeatable value and determine whether churn is driven by price or insufficient ongoing value.
Why subscription

Value is continuous

FinWise continuously monitors financial data and delivers recurring insights, so ongoing access still matches how value is delivered.
Pricing bet

Packaging matched to value

The better question is whether FinWise asks users to pay after they have experienced enough repeatable value—not whether the number should simply move up or down.
Next-stage signal

Evidence that justifies Stage 2

Repeat-value users convert and retain materially better, and conversions begin clustering around a clear value/paywall boundary.
Cancellation diagnosis: ask why users cancel, then pair the answer with the previous 30 days of behavior and billing-cycle timing. Strong engagement until renewal + price complaints suggests price churn; declining engagement before cancellation suggests value churn.

Reflection · Individual Insights

What changed in my PM thinking

Friction

A metric is not a root cause

A 2% conversion rate and 60% annual churn tell me where the problem appears, not why. The work improved when I separated observation, hypothesis, and intervention.
Aha

Activation is a measurable behavior

The strategy became concrete once Aha was expressed as events: real data connected, a meaningful insight delivered, then a second meaningful review.
Takeaway

Let evidence change the bet

My collaboration-first idea became a repeat-value-first strategy as the data sharpened the causal chain. The goal of experimentation is not to defend the first idea; it is to learn what deserves the next test.
Restraint

Do not use pricing to hide a value problem

Before changing price, diagnose whether users are leaving because the product is too expensive or because it never became indispensable.
Final thesis: FinWise should earn conversion by making financial value obvious, repeatable, and eventually collaborative. Only after that loop is proven should the team optimize the paywall or price point.
